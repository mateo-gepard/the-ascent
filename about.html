About

Who I am when I'm not building.

I was born in Munich in 2009 to a German-Georgian family, and skipped a year, so I'm the youngest in my class.

I'm in the gifted track at the Maria-Theresia-Gymnasium, taking physics as my advanced course, and I sit my Abitur in 2027. Most of what I've actually learned, though, has come from projects and from places that were willing to let a school student in — a research reactor in Garching, a makerspace at TU Munich, a competition jury that told me what I'd got wrong.

I've been publishing 3D models since I was thirteen, and they've been downloaded about ten thousand times by people I'll never meet. That's still the fact about my work I like best.

Away from the workshop

I go to the theatre and the opera a lot — eleven productions in the last two years, from Schiller and Horváth to Fidelio. I share a small cabin sailboat on the Ammersee with a friend, which in practice involves more engine maintenance than sailing. I cycle and run. I have a record player and an unreasonable attachment to it.

I cook Georgian food. In my family khinkali is how you welcome people, and I've made enough of them by now to be trusted with it.

I'm interested in watches as engineering objects rather than as things to own, which is how the trading started and why it stopped being only a business. The same goes for architecture and for anything designed to last longer than its first owner.

Places

Germany, Georgia, Italy, the United States, Greece, Austria, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and Hungary. Georgia is family rather than travel — we go back regularly, and it's the other half of where I'm from.
